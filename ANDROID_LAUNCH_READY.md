## Known Limitations on Android at Launch

These are features that Android users will NOT have compared to iOS users. Be transparent about these in any release notes or support documentation.

1. **No Apple Sign-In** -- Android users only get Google Sign-In. Devices without Google Play Services (Huawei, Amazon Fire tablets) will have no authentication option. This affects a small percentage of the target audience.

2. **No background audio (unconfirmed)** -- Background audio may not work on Android with `expo-audio`. iOS has this via `UIBackgroundModes: ["audio"]`. On Android, this requires a foreground service which is not explicitly configured. Needs physical device testing to confirm.

3. **No lock screen / notification media controls** -- iOS uses `setActiveForLockScreen` which maps to `MPNowPlayingInfoCenter`. The Android equivalent (MediaSession with foreground service notification) is not implemented. Users must open the app to control playback.

4. **No ripple feedback on buttons** -- All interactive elements use opacity press feedback instead of the Android-native Material ripple effect. Functional but feels less native.

5. **Slightly lower audio highlight precision** -- Polling interval is 100ms on Android vs 50ms on iOS. This means word-by-word audio highlighting updates at 10fps instead of 20fps. Still smooth on most devices, but budget devices may notice slightly less precise karaoke-style tracking.

6. **No headphone disconnect handling** -- When headphones are unplugged or Bluetooth disconnects, audio will switch to speaker instead of pausing automatically. iOS handles this natively.
